Mission 2 — A cultural mystery

Why is soul food more than just food?

Soul food is strongly connected with African American history and culture, especially in the American South. Its roots include food knowledge and cooking traditions developed by enslaved Africans and their descendants, who created meals from the ingredients available to them. These traditions changed over generations and became an important part of family and community life.

There is no single soul food menu. Dishes can include greens, beans, cornbread, sweet potatoes and different kinds of meat. Recipes vary between families and regions. Today, soul food is also celebrated as a source of cultural identity, creativity and memory.

Mission 3 — A tradition with more than one story

Thanksgiving is often connected with a 1621 harvest meal shared by English colonists and Wampanoag people in what is now Massachusetts. However, the popular story told to children is much simpler than the full history. The relationship between the colonists and Indigenous peoples later involved land loss, conflict and disease. For many Native Americans, Thanksgiving can be a day of mourning as well as a national holiday. Today, people celebrate it in different ways: with family meals, volunteering, travel, or by questioning the traditional story.

Reading text

B1-friendly adaptation based on a Yale News interview with food historian Paul Freedman. This is an adaptation, not the original article.

What is American food?

When people think about American food, they often imagine hamburgers, hot dogs and pizza. But American cuisine is much more interesting than this short list. Food historian Paul Freedman says that American food has been shaped by three important ideas: regional traditions, standardisation and variety.

First, different parts of the country developed their own food traditions. For example, people in Louisiana are famous for dishes such as gumbo, while New England is known for clam chowder, a thick soup made with clams. Local ingredients, climate and the people who lived in each area influenced what they cooked.

Second, food changed when factories began producing more packaged products. Canned vegetables, sliced bread and breakfast cereals became common. These foods were convenient because people could prepare meals more quickly. However, many products began to taste similar, and some local food traditions became less visible.

Third, American food is full of variety. Immigration brought new ingredients, recipes and cooking traditions to cities and towns. Italian, Chinese, Mexican and many other food traditions became part of everyday life. Some dishes also changed as cooks adapted recipes to local ingredients and tastes.

Food culture continued to change. In the 1970s, some chefs and food lovers began to pay more attention to fresh, seasonal and locally grown ingredients. Today, American food includes fast food, family recipes, regional specialities, international restaurants and modern food trends.

So, is there such a thing as American cuisine? Yes — but it is not one single dish. It is a changing mix of regional food, different communities, new ideas and old traditions.
