02
Meaning / Choose the right shade

Obligation is not prohibition.

These forms are close, but they do not mean the same thing. The difference matters in real conversations.

MUST

The speaker feels something is necessary. Often a personal opinion, strong recommendation or written instruction.

HAVE TO

Another person, a rule or the situation makes something necessary. It is an external obligation.

HAD TO

Past obligation: I had to pay for the ticket yesterday.

DON’T / DOESN’T HAVE TO

There is no obligation. It is not necessary — but it is not forbidden.

You don't have to wear a uniform = it isn't necessary.
You mustn't smoke here = it is forbidden.

03
Form / Build it correctly

Same idea. Different structures.

Remember: in questions and negatives, use do / does / did. After these auxiliaries, write have, not has.

I have to work.
She has to work.

I don't have to work.
She doesn't have to work.

Do you have to work?
Does she have to work?

We had to leave early.
Did you have to leave?

Listening / Speaking

A phone call to the driving instructor.

Read the conversation once. Then use the read-aloud button as a listening aid, practise the roles and change the details.

Dialogue / Anna books a lesson
Instructor

Hello, can I help you?

Anna

Yes, can I have some driving lessons, please?

Instructor

Of course. When can I arrange a lesson?

Anna

I'm free on Monday. We can meet at your house.

Instructor

Can you come at 8.30?

Anna

No, sorry. I can't. I have a lesson at 8.00.

Instructor

OK. Can you come at ten o'clock?

Anna

Yes, I can. The lesson is one hour, so 10.00 to 11.00 works for me.

Audio note: this uses your browser's text-to-speech voice; it is not the original course recording.

Role-play: You want to book a driving lesson, dentist appointment or language class. Ask about availability, explain when you can't come, and agree on a time. Then swap roles.
07
After class / Independent work

Take the grammar into your own life.

This task is designed for an adult learner: reflect, organise information and write for a real audience.

Mini feature · personal essay

THE RULES I LIVE BY

Create a short magazine-style feature (180–220 words) about the responsibilities, rules and expectations that shape your everyday life — at work, at home, in your studies or in your city.

Your feature must include:

  • At least 5 obligations using have to / has to.
  • At least 2 things you don't have to do.
  • 2 rules or prohibitions using mustn't / can't.
  • One past situation using had to.
  • At least 3 time expressions (e.g. every morning, once a week, last month).
  • A short reflection: which rules are useful, and which would you change?

Give it a magazine feel: add a headline, one relevant photo and 2–3 short subheadings. Avoid writing only a list — connect your ideas into a coherent text.
