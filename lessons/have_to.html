02
Meaning / Choose the right shade

Have to vs. Must: Who makes the rule?

Both have to and must express obligation or necessity, but they come from different sources — and in the negative form, their meanings are completely opposite.

1. HAVE TO — External obligation (Rules, laws, facts)

We use have to when the obligation comes from outside the speaker: a law, a boss, a teacher, a timetable, or a practical situation. You have no choice because of external circumstances.

• I have to wear a uniform at work. (It's the company rule.)
• She has to take two buses to get to university. (There is no direct train.)
2. MUST — Internal obligation (Personal feeling & strong advice)

We use must when the speaker personally feels something is necessary, urgent, or important. It is also used for strong recommendations to friends and in formal written signs/notices.

• I must call my mum tonight — I haven't spoken to her all week. (My own decision.)
• You must try that new café in Paris! (Strong recommendation.)
• Passengers must fasten their seatbelts. (Formal written rule.)
3. DON'T / DOESN'T HAVE TO — No obligation (Optional)

Use don't have to when something is not necessary. You can do it if you want to, or you can skip it — there is zero obligation.

• Tomorrow is Sunday, so I don't have to wake up early. (I can sleep in, or wake up early if I want.)
• Entrance is free — you don't have to buy a ticket.
4. MUSTN'T — Prohibition (Forbidden / Against the rules)

Use mustn't when something is strictly forbidden, dangerous, or against the rules. It means "Don't do this!"

• You mustn't park here — you'll get a fine. (It is illegal.)
• You mustn't tell anyone my secret! (I'm asking you not to do it.)

03
Form / Build it correctly

Same idea. Different structures.

Remember: must never changes (no -s, no to after it). With have to, use do / does / did in questions and negatives — and after them, always write have, never has.

I have to work. / She has to work.
I / She must leave now. (no -s, no to)

I don't have to work. / She doesn't have to work.
You mustn't tell anyone.

Do you have to work?
Does she have to work?

We had to leave early. / Did you have to leave?
You will have to book in advance.

Dialogue / Anna books a lesson
Instructor

Hello, can I help you?

Anna

Yes, can I have some driving lessons, please?

Instructor

Of course. When can I arrange a lesson?

Anna

I'm free on Monday. We can meet at your house.

Instructor

Can you come at 8.30?

Anna

No, sorry. I can't. I have a lesson at 8.00.

Instructor

OK. Can you come at ten o'clock?

Anna

Yes, I can. The lesson is one hour, so 10.00 to 11.00 works for me.

🎧 Listen to the dialogue:
Role-play: You want to book a driving lesson, dentist appointment or language class. Ask about availability, explain what you have to do at other times so you can't come, and agree on a time. Then swap roles.
