Speaking Playbook
Teacher's Guide

90-minute B2 workshop · lesson rationale, staging, question script, answer guidance, feedback and extension options.

Lesson at a glance

Learning outcomes

  • Extend Part 1 answers with relevant reasons and details.
  • Plan and deliver a coherent 1–2 minute Part 2 long turn.
  • Develop Part 3 responses through explanation, examples and qualification.
  • Use topic collocations, paraphrasing and nuanced language.
  • Reflect on performance against the four IELTS Speaking assessment criteria.

Methodological sequence

Lead-in → noticing → controlled practice → scaffolded production → freer speaking → feedback → repeat performance.

CELTA-informed rather than an official CELTA lesson plan. The student edition uses browser text-to-speech for prompts; this teacher edition provides the full written script.

01 · EXAM STRATEGY7 min

Set the purpose and activate prior knowledge

Teacher: Establish that the speaking test assesses communicative performance, not memorised content. Briefly elicit what learners know about the four criteria.

Fluency & Coherence

Length and continuity of speech; logical organisation and cohesive devices.

Lexical Resource

Range, precision, collocation and ability to paraphrase.

Grammatical Range & Accuracy

Variety and control of grammatical structures.

Pronunciation

Intelligibility, word/sentence stress, rhythm and intonation.

Warm-up prompt — read aloud

QuestionTell me about a skill you use regularly in your everyday life.

Procedure: Give 20 seconds thinking time; learner speaks for 30 seconds. Do not interrupt. Ask a follow-up only if needed: “When did you first develop this skill?”

Listen for: direct answer, development, example, hesitation patterns.

02 · SPEAKING PART 111 min

Short questions, developed answers

Board/teach: Direct answer → reason → small example/detail. Emphasise that this is a flexible scaffold, not a rigid formula. Part 1 responses should remain concise and conversational.

Question script · Topic: free time

Q1What do you usually do when you have a free afternoon?
Q2Do you prefer spending your free time indoors or outdoors? Why?
Q3Has the way you spend your free time changed in recent years?

Possible follow-up prompts

  • What do you enjoy about that?
  • How often do you do it?
  • Would you like to spend more time doing it?

Answer comparison

Underdeveloped

“I like studying in cafés because they are good.”

Possible upgrade

“I quite enjoy studying in cafés because the change of environment helps me concentrate. I usually choose a quiet place with natural light, especially when I have to read for a long time.”

Feedback focus: Was the answer relevant? Did the learner explain why? Was there a concrete detail? Avoid correcting every error; select one high-impact improvement.

03 · VOCABULARY TOOLKIT10 min

Lexical input and retrieval practice

Procedure: Learners categorise chunks, choose four they can use authentically, then personalise them in sentences. Elicit pronunciation and collocation patterns.

Free time & lifestyle

unwind after work · make time for · get into a routine · have a packed schedule · take up a hobby · switch off mentally · strike a balance · be short on time

Places & cities

within walking distance · a lively atmosphere · off the beaten track · a hidden gem · well-connected · overcrowded · green spaces · urban sprawl

Learning & work

pick up a skill · learn through trial and error · meet a deadline · build confidence · hands-on experience · keep up with · make steady progress · step outside my comfort zone

Opinions & discussion

from my perspective · to a certain extent · a major contributing factor · there is a trade-off · worth bearing in mind · in the long run · that said · benefits outweigh the drawbacks

Controlled practice answer key

  1. unwind
  2. within walking distance
  3. trial and error
  4. trade-off

Extension: Ask learners to create a true example using each chunk, then ask a partner one follow-up question.

04 · SPEAKING PART 216 min

One-minute planning and long turn

Cue cardDescribe a time when you did something for the first time.

You should say:
  • what you did
  • when and where it happened
  • who was with you, if anyone
  • and explain how you felt about the experience.

Planning scaffold: context · action · vivid detail · reflection. Learners write keywords only. Allow exactly one minute.

Useful language to elicit

It all started when… · At first, I was a little… · As it turned out,… · What made it memorable was… · Looking back, I realise… · It was a learning curve. · Despite the initial… · By the end of it,…

First performance: 1–2 minutes. Listener notes one strength and one area for development; no interruption.

Second performance: Repeat the same card. Require one specific upgrade (more precise verbs, a clearer timeline or reflective comment). This creates an opportunity to notice improvement.

Suggested story organisation

  1. Introduce the event and context.
  2. Explain what happened in chronological order.
  3. Include one vivid or unexpected detail.
  4. Reflect on the experience and feelings.
05 · SPEAKING PART 313 min

Abstract discussion and nuanced opinions

Teach the IDEA ladder: Idea → Develop → Example → Add nuance. Model one answer, then let learners try without reading.

Question script — read aloud, one at a time

Q1Why do some people find it difficult to try new experiences?
Q2Do you think schools should give students more opportunities to learn practical skills?
Q3How can cities encourage people to spend more time in public spaces?

Additional question bank

Q4Why are some lessons more memorable than others?
Q5Do people learn more effectively from people or from technology?
Q6Should employers be responsible for helping staff develop new skills?
Q7How might education change over the next twenty years?

Useful language

One explanation might be… · This tends to happen when… · A case in point is… · Compared with previous generations,… · It would be an oversimplification to say… · There are two sides to this. · It largely depends on… · In the long term,… · Having said that,… · One possible downside is…

Feedback: Focus on whether the learner actually develops the point. A list of opinions is not the same as a developed argument.

06 · PRONUNCIATION LAB8 min

Intelligibility, stress and chunking

Model sentence: “Although I enjoy travelling alone, I sometimes prefer going with friends because sharing an experience makes it more memorable.”

Procedure: Teacher models once; learners mark thought groups, identify prominent content words, shadow the model and record their own version.

Checklist: key content-word stress · meaningful thought groups · controlled pace · clear final consonants. Do not treat accent elimination as the goal.

07 · FULL SPEAKING PRACTICE15 min

Mini mock test — complete script

Part 1 · Introduction and interview

Q1Do you work or are you a student?
Q2What do you find most satisfying about your work or studies?
Q3Do you prefer working on your own or with other people?
Q4What would you like to learn in the future?

Part 2 · Individual long turn

Cue cardDescribe a person who has taught you something important.

You should say:
  • who this person is
  • what they taught you
  • how they taught you
  • and explain why this lesson has been important to you.

Allow 1 minute to prepare and 1–2 minutes to speak.

Part 3 · Discussion

Q1Why are some lessons more memorable than others?
Q2Do people learn more effectively from people or from technology?
Q3Should employers be responsible for helping staff develop new skills?
Q4How might education change over the next twenty years?
Mock test protocol: Ask one question at a time. Do not teach or correct during the test. Avoid excessive prompting. Take notes on evidence for the four criteria and give feedback afterwards.
08 · FEEDBACK & HOMEWORK10 min

Feedback and independent practice

Feedback sequence

  1. Ask learner what they felt went well.
  2. Give one strength with a concrete example from their speech.
  3. Choose one priority for improvement.
  4. Model an improved version and ask learner to repeat it.
  5. Agree one measurable homework target.

Homework (35–45 min)

Part 2 cue card: Describe a decision you made that had a positive effect on your life.

You should say what the decision was, when you made it, what influenced you and why it was positive.

  • Record a 1–2 minute answer.
  • Listen and identify 3 strong expressions, 2 hesitations/repetitions and 1 pronunciation point.
  • Record the same answer again after making improvements; compare the two.
  • Prepare 40–60 second answers to: “Why do people sometimes regret important decisions?” and “Should young people be encouraged to take risks?”
  • Learn 10 lexical chunks: add a definition/synonym and an original sentence.
