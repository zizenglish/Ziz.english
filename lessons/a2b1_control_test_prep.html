Module 01 • Explanation & Practice

First & Second Conditionals

12 min

How to Choose Between First and Second Conditional

First Conditional (Real Future Possibility)

If + Present Simple, will / can / may + V1

Use for realistic situations in the future and their likely result.

• If it rains tomorrow, we will stay at home.
• If she passes the test, her parents will buy her a bike.

Second Conditional (Imaginary / Unlikely)

If + Past Simple, would / could + V1

Use for unreal, imaginary, or very unlikely situations now or in the future.

• If I had a million euros, I would travel around the world.
• If I were you, I would revise tonight. (advice)

Control Test Rule: Never put will or would inside the If-clause! (✗ If I will see him → ✓ If I see him; ✗ If I would have time → ✓ If I had time).

Complex Sentences with when, unless, as

12 min

How when, unless, and as Connect Clauses

Word Meaning & Grammar Rule Example Sentence
unless Means if ... not. Followed by an affirmative verb. Unless you book a table, we won't get a seat. (= If you don't book...)
when Means at the time that (sure to happen). Use Present Simple for future meaning. Text me when you arrive at the airport. (NOT: when you will arrive)
as 1) while / at the same time
2) because / since		 1) As I was walking home, it started to snow.
2) As it was late, we took a taxi.
Punctuation Tip: When the subordinate clause (When / Unless / As...) comes first, put a comma after it. When it comes second, no comma is needed.

Relative Clauses & Subordinate Clauses

12 min

Understanding Defining, Non-Defining & Subordinate Clauses

1. Defining Relative Clauses (No Commas)

Give essential information to show which person, thing, or place we mean:

• who / that → people (The boy who won is 14.)
• which / that → things/animals (The book which I bought...)
• where → places (The café where we met...)
• whose → possession (The girl whose bag was lost...)

2. Non-Defining & Subordinate Clauses

Non-defining clauses add extra info between commas:

• My uncle, who lives in Lviv, is an architect.
• Kyiv, which stands on the Dnipro, is very green.

Subordinate clauses (time, reason, contrast) attach to a main clause using because, although, when, while.

Control Test Rule: After a comma in a non-defining clause, you can use who or which, but you can NEVER use "that"!

Modal Verbs: must, have to, need, should, might

12 min

Obligation, Prohibition, No Necessity, Advice & Possibility

Modal / Form Meaning Example
must / have to + V1 Strong obligation or rule (повинен / мусиш) You must / have to wear a seatbelt in the car.
mustn't + V1 Prohibition — strictly forbidden (заборонено) You mustn't use your phone during the test.
don't have to / needn't + V1 No obligation — not necessary (не обов'язково) You don't have to / needn't print the ticket.
need to + V1 vs. needn't + V1 need to takes to; modal needn't takes no to I need to leave early. / You needn't worry.
should / shouldn't + V1 Advice or recommendation (варто / не варто) You should get eight hours of sleep before an exam.
might / may + V1 Possibility / uncertainty (можливо) Take a sweater—it might get cold later.
#1 Exam Trap: mustn't (заборонено) and don't have to / needn't (не потрібно) have completely different meanings! Also never say "needn't to go".

Adjectives Ending in -ed vs. -ing

12 min

Feeling (-ed) vs. Cause of the Feeling (-ing)

Adjectives ending in -ed → HOW SOMEONE FEELS

Describes a person's (or animal's) emotional state:

• I felt bored during the lecture.
• She was amazed by the concert.
• They were confused by the map.

Adjectives ending in -ing → WHAT CAUSES THE FEELING

Describes the thing, event, or person creating that emotion:

• The lecture was boring.
• The concert was amazing.
• The map was confusing.

Quick Test: Ask yourself: "Is this how someone feels inside?" → -ed. "Is this describing a thing/situation (or what a person is like to others)?" → -ing (e.g., an interesting teacher).
