Chapter II · Grammar Architecture & Clinic 15 MIN

Future in the Past + Contrast Linkers

Study how past-plan forms and contrast linkers work together on the IELTS exam, then complete the 10-item Grammar Clinic.

Future in the Past (Plan Signals)

Used when a speaker describes an earlier intention, schedule, or design that changed or did not happen as expected:

was / were going to + V1
Personal intention or decision prior to a change.
“We were going to cycle along the canal, but it rained.”
was / were supposed to + V1
Scheduled time, rule, or arrangement that shifted.
“The coach was supposed to leave at nine.”
was / were meant to + V1
Intended purpose or original design (common in Part 2 talks).
“The old annex was meant to be a café.”

Contrast Linkers (Pivot Signals)

Used to contrast conditions, concede a point, or compare two different facts in Part 2 and Part 3:

although / though + Subject + Verb
Followed by a full clause. Though can also sit at the end of a sentence.
“Although tickets were limited, we got two seats.”
despite / in spite of + Noun / V-ing
Followed by a noun phrase or -ing form (never despite of; never direct S + V clause).
“Despite the fog, the ferry sailed on schedule.”
whereas + Subject + Verb
Contrasts two distinct facts or viewpoints side by side.
“Liam prefers lab work, whereas Chloe likes field notes.”

Clinic Part B · B1 → B2 Sentence Transformations (4 Items)

Rewrite the target chunk using the cue word in BOLD.

1. Although the team had a tight budget, they completed the survey on time. (DESPITE)
→ , they completed the survey on time.
2. The field briefing was scheduled to start at 8:30, but the bus ran late. (SUPPOSED)
→ The field briefing at 8:30, but the bus ran late.
3. Surveys give broad data. Interviews give deeper personal context. (WHEREAS)
→ Surveys give broad data, deeper personal context.
4. Planners originally intended the West Wing to be a reading room. (MEANT)
→ The West Wing a reading room.

Part 2: Ashbourne Botanical Park & Visitor Briefing (Q1–Q10)

Listen to the park ranger's briefing and answer Questions 1–5 (Multiple Choice) and Questions 6–10 (Facility Matching).

SIGNAL STANDBY // Part 2 Monologue (Q1–Q10)

“Good morning, everyone, and welcome to Ashbourne Botanical Park! Before we set off, here are a few important updates. We were supposed to meet at the Main Gate at ten o'clock, although drainage repairs have closed that entrance this week, so we are gathering beside the Conservatory instead.

Many visitors asked about hiring electric park scooters today. The park was going to introduce thirty electric scooters this spring, despite battery delivery delays. Whereas electric scooters arrive next month, standard push-bikes are available to rent right now at the cycle kiosk.

Also, the afternoon tree-identification walk has moved. It was meant to begin in the Oak Glade, yet over eighty guests joined, so we shifted it to the wider Meadow Trail. And if you want a printed park guide, we were going to hand them out free at the ticket booth, though printing costs meant they are now two pounds at the gift shop.

For buses returning to the town centre, route 14 was supposed to run every fifteen minutes all afternoon, whereas roadwork on Mill Lane means buses run every thirty minutes from the South Stop.

Finally, let's check five park areas on your map. The Palm House was meant to close at four o'clock, although summer hours keep it open until seven. The Alpine House was going to hold a seed exhibition, whereas it is currently undergoing glass restoration. Near the lake, despite narrow paths, volunteers lead morning bird-watching walks. The Lakeside Café was supposed to serve hot meals all day, yet today it offers cold wraps and drinks only. And in the Orchard Yard, which was meant to be a parking bay, we now host outdoor sculpture displays.”

A At the Main Gate
B At the ticket booth
C Beside the Conservatory
Trap vs. Truth: “were supposed to meet at the Main Gate” is cancelled due to drainage repairs; the real meeting point is beside the Conservatory (C).
2. What can visitors rent at the cycle kiosk today?
A Electric park scooters
B Standard push-bikes
C Tandem bikes for four people
Trap vs. Truth: Electric scooters are delayed until next month; standard push-bikes are available now (B).

A More than eighty visitors joined the group.
B Tree branches fell across the Oak Glade.
C The walk was rescheduled for tomorrow.
Trap vs. Truth: “was meant to begin in the Oak Glade” changed because over eighty guests joined (A).
4. How much does a printed park guide cost at the gift shop?
A Free of charge
B £2
C £5
Trap vs. Truth: They were going to be free at the ticket booth, but are now £2 at the gift shop (B).
5. How often do route 14 buses leave the South Stop this afternoon?
A Every 15 minutes
B Every 20 minutes
C Every 30 minutes
Trap vs. Truth: Every 15 minutes was the old schedule; whereas roadworks mean buses run every 30 minutes (C).

Part 3: Urban Commuting Research Tutorial (Q11–Q18)

Listen to three voices — Sarah, Leo, and Dr. Evans — planning a study on city commuting habits. Answer Questions 11–18.

SIGNAL STANDBY // Three-Speaker Academic Discussion (Q11–Q18)

Dr. Evans: Good afternoon, Sarah and Leo. Tell me how your urban commuting project is shaping up.

Sarah: Afternoon, Dr. Evans. We were going to focus on electric scooters, although we found out another group is already surveying scooter riders, so we switched to commuter rail delays instead.

Dr. Evans: Smart move. How will you gather responses?

Leo: I prefer short platform interviews, whereas Sarah wants to run a digital poll so we can reach more commuters quickly.

Dr. Evans: Both can work if your sample is realistic. You were supposed to collect 250 responses by Friday, though with only four days left, aiming for 75 commuters is much more realistic.

Sarah: Agreed. Yesterday we tested a draft questionnaire. It was meant to take four minutes, whereas participants averaged nine minutes because the wording was too long.

Leo: Despite the extra editing work, we will cut the survey to eight short questions before sending it out, and we will present our results as a slide deck rather than a printed booklet.

Questions 11–14 · Multiple Choice (A, B, or C)

11. Why did Sarah and Leo change their research topic?
A Commuter rail services were on strike.
B Another group was already studying electric scooters.
C Dr. Evans asked them to stop all fieldwork.
Trap vs. Truth: Electric scooters was the initial idea, but another group already took it; they switched to commuter rail delays (B).
12. How do Leo and Sarah's initial research preferences contrast?
A Leo wanted a digital poll, whereas Sarah wanted paper forms.
B Sarah wanted phone calls, whereas Leo wanted email surveys.
C Leo favoured platform interviews, whereas Sarah wanted a digital poll.
Trap vs. Truth: Leo liked platform interviews, whereas Sarah preferred a digital poll (C).
13. Why did the draft questionnaire take longer than expected?
A The wording of the questions was too long.
B The survey website crashed on mobile phones.
C Participants could not read the small print.
Trap vs. Truth: “was meant to take four minutes”; it took nine because the wording was too long (A).
14. How will they present their final results?
A As a printed booklet
B As a slide deck
C As a radio documentary
Trap vs. Truth: They will use a slide deck rather than a printed booklet (B).
