Stage 2 • Future in the Past & Contrast Linkers 15 MIN

Grammar Toolkit: Cancelled Plans & Contrast Pivots

Review how Future in the Past and Contrast Linkers signal exam distractors, then complete the 10-item practice below.

Future in the Past (Changed Plans)

Shows an earlier plan, schedule, or design that changed or didn't happen:

was / were going to + V1
Personal intention before a change: “We were going to cycle, but it rained.”
was / were supposed to + V1
Scheduled time or rule that shifted: “The bus was supposed to arrive at 9:00.”
was / were meant to + V1
Intended purpose/design: “This wing was meant to be a café.”

Contrast Linkers (Listening Pivots)

Alerts you that the speaker is contrasting ideas or shifting to the real fact:

although / even though / though + S + V
Followed by a full clause: “Although tickets were pricey, we went.”
despite / in spite of + Noun / V-ing
Followed by noun or -ing (never despite of): “Despite the rain, we walked.”
whereas + S + V
Contrasts two facts/opinions: “Tom likes surveys, whereas Mia prefers interviews.”

Part A · Gap-Fill Check (6 Items)

1. _____ the heavy rain, the afternoon walking tour went ahead.
2. The lecture _____ start at 10:00, though a train delay made it ten minutes late.
3. Morning sessions are lectures, _____ afternoon sessions are group workshops.
4. In spite of _____ early, they still missed the first train.
5. We _____ book the small room, but too many students signed up.
6. _____ the museum was busy, we enjoyed the new exhibition.

Part B · Sentence Transformations (4 Items)

1. Although the club had a small budget, they organized a great trip. (DESPITE)
→ , they organized a great trip.
2. The gallery was scheduled to open at nine, but staff had a meeting. (SUPPOSED)
→ The gallery at nine.
3. Online surveys are fast. Face-to-face interviews give more detail. (WHEREAS)
→ Online surveys are fast, more detail.
4. The old hall was originally intended to be a student lounge. (MEANT)
→ The old hall a student lounge.

Part 2: Botanical Park Orientation Briefing (Q1–Q10)

Listen to the park guide's briefing and complete Questions 1–5 (Multiple Choice) and Questions 6–10 (Facility Matching).

PODCAST PLAYER READY // Part 2 Monologue (Q1–Q10)

“Welcome to Ashbourne Botanical Park! We were supposed to meet at the Main Gate at ten, although drainage work closed that gate, so please gather by the Conservatory instead.

Many guests asked about electric scooters. The park was going to launch thirty electric scooters this spring, despite battery delays. Whereas e-scooters arrive next month, standard push-bikes are available now at the kiosk.

The tree walk was meant to start in the Oak Glade, yet eighty people joined, so we moved it to the Meadow Trail. Printed guides were going to be free at the booth, though now they cost two pounds at the gift shop. Shuttle buses were supposed to run every fifteen minutes, whereas roadworks mean they run every thirty minutes from the South Stop.

Finally, five park spots: the Palm House stays open until seven; the Alpine House was going to host a seed show, whereas it is being restored; near the lake, despite narrow paths, volunteers run bird walks; the Lakeside Café was supposed to serve hot meals all day, yet today it serves cold wraps only; and the Orchard Yard was meant to be a car park, whereas it now displays sculptures.”

Part 3: Commuting Study Discussion (Q11–Q18)

Listen to Sarah, Leo, and Dr. Evans discussing their research project, then answer Questions 11–18.

PODCAST PLAYER READY // Part 3 Dialogue (Q11–Q18)

Dr. Evans: Hi Sarah and Leo. How is your commuting study going?

Sarah: We were going to study electric scooters, although another group already picked that topic, so we switched to train delays instead.

Leo: I prefer short station interviews, whereas Sarah wants an online poll for faster data.

Dr. Evans: You were supposed to survey 250 people, though with four days left, 75 commuters is realistic.

Sarah: Our test survey was meant to take four minutes, whereas people took nine minutes because the questions were too long.

Leo: Despite extra editing, we will cut it to eight short questions and present our results as slides.

Questions 11–14 · Multiple Choice (A, B, or C)

11. Why did Sarah and Leo change their topic?
A Trains were cancelled all week.
B Another group was already studying electric scooters.
C They ran out of survey budget.
Explanation: Another group already chose scooters; they switched to train delays (B).
12. What is the contrast between Leo and Sarah's methods?
A Leo wants a poll, whereas Sarah wants interviews.
B Both want paper questionnaires.
C Leo prefers station interviews, whereas Sarah wants an online poll.
Explanation: Leo prefers station interviews, whereas Sarah wants an online poll (C).
13. Why did the test survey take nine minutes?
A The questions were too long.
B The link stopped working.
C Commuters had no internet.
Explanation: It was meant to take four minutes, but took nine because the questions were too long (A).
14. How will they present their findings?
A In a printed report
B As slides
C In a podcast
Explanation: They will present their results as slides (B).
