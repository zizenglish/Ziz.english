Stage 2 • Practical Brainstorming (5 Minutes in Exam) 15 Minutes

How to Generate Ideas + Examples Fast

Every IELTS body paragraph needs: 1. Main Idea → 2. Why it happens → 3. Specific Example. Fill in the table below.

View 1: Computers & Internet Are More Important

Idea 1 (Unlimited Access): Students can access global knowledge, video lectures, and interactive apps anytime, regardless of where they live.

Example 1: A teenager in a rural village can learn coding or foreign languages on platforms like Khan Academy or Coursera for free.

Idea 2 (Self-Paced Learning): Every child learns at a different speed; online tools let students pause, replay, and practice weak areas.

Example 2: Language apps adapt difficulty automatically when a student makes grammar mistakes.

View 2: Schools & Teachers Are Essential

Idea 1 (Personalised Feedback & Motivation): Children lack self-discipline; human teachers spot confusion immediately and keep students motivated.

Example 1: When a primary pupil struggles with fractions, a teacher can explain the concept differently, whereas a computer cannot sense frustration.

Idea 2 (Socialisation & Teamwork): School teaches communication, empathy, and conflict resolution—skills impossible to learn alone behind a screen.

Example 2: Group science projects and school sports teach children how to cooperate with peers.

Stage 6 • Authentic Band 8.5 Model Script 12 Minutes

Model Essay Analysis (Band 8.5 • 274 Words)

Click the buttons to highlight the Topic Sentences, Connectors, and Specific Examples in a real high-level IELTS essay.

While some people argue that computers and the Internet play a greater role in a child's education than attending school, others contend that physical classrooms and teachers remain indispensable. Although digital tools offer valuable flexibility, I firmly believe that school-based learning under a teacher's guidance is far more effective for children's overall development.

On the one hand, proponents of online learning point out that the Internet provides unmatched access to educational resources. Unlike traditional textbooks, digital platforms offer interactive simulations, video tutorials, and self-paced courses that allow children to explore subjects in depth. For instance, a teenager living in a remote area with limited school facilities can learn computer programming or foreign languages online free of charge. Consequently, technology can democratise knowledge and support independent study outside classroom hours.

On the other hand, I am convinced that schools and qualified teachers are irreplaceable because young learners require structured guidance and socialisation. Children often lack the self-discipline to study effectively alone behind a screen, whereas an attentive teacher can immediately identify confusion and adapt their explanations. A clear illustration of this is when a primary school pupil struggles with basic mathematics; a teacher can offer tailored encouragement, which an automated programme cannot replicate. Furthermore, attending school teaches vital interpersonal skills—such as teamwork, empathy, and conflict resolution—through daily face-to-face interaction with peers.

In conclusion, while computers and the Internet are powerful supplementary tools, they cannot replace the human mentorship and social environment of a traditional school.

❌ Weak Band 6.0 Paragraph

"Teachers are very important for kids. At school kids have friends and teachers explain everything. If a kid studies only on a computer, they will play games and be lazy. So schools are better than the Internet."

Why Band 6.0: Informal word "kids", unsupported generalisation ("will play games"), no specific example.

✓ Why the Model Above Scores Band 8.5
  • • Clear 4-paragraph structure: Intro → View 1 → View 2 (Writer's Opinion) → Conclusion.
  • • Natural linkers: On the one hand, For instance, Consequently, Whereas, Furthermore.
  • • Specific examples: Remote teenager learning coding; primary pupil struggling with maths.
