GRAMMAR NOTES

Two ways to show family connections

Read and notice the patterns. No exercise here — just clear examples.

Possessive ’s

Lizzie’s mother = the mother of Lizzie

We add ’s to a name or singular noun to show possession or a family relationship.

my mum’s sister
Mark’s children

For regular plural nouns ending in -s, add only an apostrophe: my parents’ friends.

Subject pronouns + possessive adjectives

SubjectPossessiveExample
Imymy shirt
youyouryour book
hehishis pillow
sheherher dog
ititsits ball
weourour bird
theytheirtheir car
Remember: possessive adjectives come before a noun: her name, their house, our family. Its (no apostrophe) shows possession. It’s means it is.

GRAMMAR NOTES + PRACTICE

Subject pronouns

Who does the action? Use a subject pronoun.

Subject pronouns

I need help.

You need help.

He needs help.

She needs help.

It needs help.

We need help.

They need help.

Look at the difference

Maria is my mum. → She is my mum.

Mark and Maria are parents. → They are parents.

My cat is small. → It is small.

My brother and I are students. → We are students.

FINAL SPEAKING TASK

Tell me about this family

Look at the picture prompt and describe the family.
A family spending time together

Who can you see? What are their relationships? What are their names?

Try to include:

  • at least 5 family words
  • at least 3 possessive adjectives: my, his, her, our, their
  • at least 3 subject pronouns: he, she, they, we
Plan your description

Type a few ideas. Then use them to speak for 30–45 seconds.

TAKE-HOME TASK

Your family mini-album

A little project to practise after the lesson.

Create one page about a family

  1. Draw a small family tree. You can use your real family, an imaginary family or characters from a film.
  2. Write 6–8 sentences about the people.
  3. Use at least 6 family words, 4 possessive adjectives and 3 subject pronouns.
  4. Record yourself reading your text (30–60 seconds).
Sentence starters:

These are my… · His name is… · Her sister is… · Their children are… · We are… · They live…

Bonus challenge: include one extended-family word, for example cousin, niece, nephew, aunt, uncle or mother-in-law.
