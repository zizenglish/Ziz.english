Why I Stopped Trying to “Romanticize” Every Second of My Life

Last year, my camera roll looked like a Pinterest board, even though my real life felt exhausting. I used to be obsessed with looking aesthetic online, and every weekend my best friend and I would spend two hours editing photos of our morning matcha just to curate the perfect feed. Whenever a new trend appeared, I felt huge peer pressure to buy the right notebook, wear the right oversized sweater, and keep up with every viral video. Looking good online started to feel like an unpaid full-time job.

The turning point happened in November during exam week. I was filming a “study with me” clip at midnight when I accidentally knocked my iced matcha all over my chemistry notes—and burst into tears from pure burnout. That night, I realized that chasing a flawless online image was completely overrated. I was so busy trying to romanticize my routine for strangers that I wasn't actually enjoying my real friendships.

After that night, I deleted three editing apps and decided to take a break from posting. At first, it felt strange—I wasn't used to having quiet, phone-free weekends, and I kept reaching for my camera out of habit. However, after a couple of weeks, I slowly got used to living in the moment without filming everything. Now I am completely used to spending Sunday afternoons with a real book and zero notifications. My style isn't one trendy label anymore—it's just authentic to me, and that feels like real main character energy.

Part B • Visual Scrapbook Cheat Sheet (All 4 Structures)

Notice the crucial difference: the top two talk about the PAST ONLY (+ base verb), while the bottom two talk about FAMILIARITY / ADAPTING (+ -ing / noun) in any tense!

used to + V1 (base verb)

1. used to (Past Habits & States)

Talks about repeated actions AND states/feelings in the past that are no longer true today.

(+) I used to watch beauty vlogs every night.
(+) She used to have long pink hair. (past state)
(−) I didn't use to like thrift shops. (no -d!)
(?) Did you use to collect stickers?
✨ Works with BOTH: Action verbs (play, go, edit) AND State verbs (be, have, live, love, believe).
would + V1 (base verb)

2. would (Nostalgic Past Actions Only)

Talks about repeated actions and nostalgic routines in the past (often with a past time frame like “When I was 12...”).

✅ Every summer, we would stay up until 2 AM talking.
✅ She would always take polaroids at parties.
❌ I would have a pink bedroom. → Use used to have!
❌ He would be very shy. → Use used to be!
⚠️ Golden Rule: NEVER use would with state verbs (be, have, like, know, live) for past states!
am / is / are / was + used to + V-ing / noun

3. be used to (“It's Normal For Me”)

Means something is already familiar, easy, and normal for you because you have done it many times. Here, to is a preposition!

Present: I am used to waking up at 6:30 AM.
Negative: She isn't used to cold weather yet.
Past: At first, I wasn't used to living in a big city.
With noun: We are used to the noise now.
💡 Why -ing? Because used is an adjective here, and to is a preposition (just like good at swimming).
get / getting / got + used to + V-ing / noun

4. get used to (“Becoming Normal”)

Focuses on the process of adapting — something was strange or difficult at first, and you are slowly learning to feel comfortable with it.

Present Cont.: I am getting used to studying in English.
Past Simple: It took a week, but I got used to my new school.
Future: Don't worry, you will get used to it soon!
Negative: I can't get used to wearing contact lenses.
🔄 Compare: I'm getting used to it (in progress) → I am used to it (100% normal now!).
🕰️ Group 1: Past Habits & States (+ Base Verb V1) used to + V1 (actions & states)
would + V1 (repeated actions ONLY — no state verbs!)
🌱 Group 2: Familiarity & Adapting (+ V-ing / Noun) be used to + V-ing (state: it is already normal)
get used to + V-ing (process: it is becoming normal)

How a 30-Day Thrift Challenge Changed My Style

Six months ago, I used to order fast-fashion clothes online every month because I wanted to keep up with viral haul videos. Every weekend I would scroll through shopping apps for hours, yet half of those outfits were poor quality and I only wore them once! In April, my cousin challenged me to avoid new stores for 30 days and only shop second-hand. At first, I wasn't used to searching through crowded thrift racks, and it felt a bit overwhelming. However, after two visits I got used to spotting hidden vintage gems—and I even found a classic denim jacket for €10. Now I'm completely used to styling second-hand pieces, and my wardrobe feels way more authentic than copying a trend.
