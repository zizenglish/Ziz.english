01

Think beyond the obvious

Start with a dilemma, not a vocabulary list.

10 min
“If a product is free, you might not be the customer. You might be the product.”

Imagine a social media platform offers a free service. In return, it studies user behaviour, predicts what people will click on and tries to keep them online for as long as possible.

Warm-up: four questions

Agree or disagree?

Choose a position and justify it aloud. Encourage the student to qualify their answer rather than give an absolute response.

Challenge your assumptions

“Social media is harmful because people spend too much time on it.” What is missing from this argument?

Teacher guidance · reveal after discussion

Look for the difference between correlation and causation. Time spent online does not automatically demonstrate harm. Ask what content is consumed, what outcomes are affected, whether platform design matters, and whether social media can also provide support, education and connection.

Objective: move from a general opinion to a qualified, defensible position.

02

Build knowledge and arguments

Use evidence carefully; distinguish a plausible claim from a proven fact.

12 min

Four dimensions of the debate

PsychologicalConcentration, habit formation, fear of missing out, sleep disruption.
EconomicAdvertising revenue, consumer behaviour, productivity, competition for attention.
SocialConnection, community building, social isolation, access to information.
Ethical and politicalPrivacy, freedom of choice, corporate responsibility, regulation.

Evidence briefing

1 · Digital life is widespread

Pew Research Center publishes surveys on internet and social media use, including differences by age group. Use these to establish context, not to claim that every user experiences harm.

Explore Pew Research Center: Internet & Technology ↗
2 · Digital well-being is complex

WHO resources on mental health provide context for discussing well-being. Avoid assuming that one simple relationship explains all digital behaviour.

Explore WHO: Mental health ↗
3 · Platform design is a policy issue

The European Commission's Digital Services Act framework includes obligations relating to transparency and systemic risks of online platforms, with additional protections for minors.

Explore the Digital Services Act ↗
4 · Attention has commercial value

Advertising-funded platforms may have incentives to increase engagement. This is an economic argument about incentives; it should not be presented as proof that all design choices are intentionally addictive.

Explore the US Federal Trade Commission ↗
Evidence rule for IELTS: a relevant, logically explained example is better than an invented statistic. If you use a statistic, know its source, date and population.

Argument-building: six statements

Teacher prompts if the student gets stuck
  • What mechanism connects the cause and the consequence?
  • Who is affected most?
  • Under what circumstances might the opposite be true?
  • Can you give a realistic example?
  • What is the strongest counterargument?

Upgrade “Social media wastes time” to: “Engagement-driven recommendation systems may encourage users to remain online longer than they originally intended.” Then ask the learner to explain the mechanism and qualify the claim.

03

Academic vocabulary upgrade

Precision and natural collocation matter more than impressive-sounding words.

15 min

Vocabulary quiz

Choose the most appropriate expression. Check only after answering all five.

Complete the sentences

Find and repair unnatural collocations

  1. Social media can have a detrimental effect on mental well-being.
  2. Governments should regulate online platforms.
  3. Excessive screen time can undermine concentration.
  4. Recommendation systems may reinforce existing beliefs.

Sentence transformation workshop · 8 challenges

Upgrade the sentence without changing its central meaning. Try more precise vocabulary, a clear mechanism or a complex but controlled structure.

Nuance check

Explain the difference: (1) Recommendation systems reinforce existing beliefs. (2) Recommendation systems exacerbate existing beliefs.

Teacher note

Reinforce means strengthen something already present. Exacerbate means make a problem or negative condition worse. “Exacerbate existing beliefs” is usually less natural unless the beliefs themselves are framed as a problem.

04

Understand the IELTS task

Read the instruction precisely before planning your position.

10 min
IELTS-style question

Social media and responsibility

Some people believe that social media companies should be held responsible for the negative effects of their platforms on users' behaviour and mental well-being. Others argue that individuals should take responsibility for how they use these platforms.

Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience. Write at least 250 words.

Deconstruct the question

Teacher explanation

This is a two-sided discussion question with an explicit opinion. A complete response must discuss both perspectives and make the writer's own position clear. Merely mentioning both sides is not enough: each needs explanation and support.

What each essay element does

ElementPurposeExample
IntroductionParaphrase the issue and establish the position.“The debate centres on whether…”
Thesis statementAnswer the question and signal the argument.“Although individuals must exercise self-control, platforms should bear greater responsibility for harmful design.”
Topic sentenceState the paragraph's controlling idea.“Those who favour individual responsibility argue that…”
ExplanationShow how or why the point is true.“This is because users can…”
ExampleMake the mechanism concrete and relevant.A student disabling non-essential notifications during study.
ImplicationExplain why the point matters to the question.“This suggests that personal agency remains important.”
ConclusionSummarise the position without introducing a new argument.“Ultimately, responsibility should be shared, but…”

Authentic Task 2 · Band 5.5

Candidate Response 1

“I believe that most of the people in the world have more information about their health…”

Official examiner comment: the position is clear and relevant ideas are present, but they need further development; the response is also below the 250-word minimum.

Annotation investigation

  • Task Response: position is clear, but ideas need fuller development and the response is under-length.
  • Coherence & Cohesion: general progression exists, but paragraphing is not always logical.
  • Lexical Resource: vocabulary range is attempted, but word choice, spelling and word formation lack precision.
  • Grammar: a mix of sentence forms appears, but the error level limits the score.

Teaching point: having an opinion is not the same as developing it sufficiently.

Authentic Task 2 · Band 7.5

Candidate Response 2

“In my opinion, I think that people will become unhealthier in the future than they are now.”

Official examiner comment: a clear position is presented; ideas are logically organised and there is a clear progression. The examiner notes some overuse of sequencers and opportunities for more effective paragraphing.

Annotation investigation

  • Task Response: clear position; supporting ideas explored; an alternative position is considered and rejected.
  • Coherence & Cohesion: logical progression, but sequencers such as “Firstly”, “So” and “Also” are overused in one paragraph.
  • Lexical Resource: a wide range, including less common vocabulary and softening expressions.
  • Grammar: varied complex structures with frequent error-free sentences, although some short forms are overused.

Teaching point: a Band 7.5 response is not flawless. Examiners identify strengths and specific limitations.

How to run the investigation
  1. Ask the student to read the full scripts in the official PDF.
  2. Have them mark the thesis, topic sentences, examples, concessions and conclusion.
  3. Ask them to underline collocations and circle linking devices.
  4. Only then reveal the examiner-based analysis.

Because the official examples are on health rather than the lesson topic, ask the learner to transfer the observed techniques to the social-media prompt.

Band 9: what can be verified?

Use the official IELTS public band descriptors as the Band 9 benchmark, not a fabricated sample essay. At Band 9, the response fully addresses the task; progression is effortless; vocabulary is flexible, precise and natural; and grammatical control is exceptionally strong.

06

Band 6.5 vs Band 8/9

A teaching comparison written for this lesson, not official candidate scripts.

8 min

Both paragraphs answer the same question. These are illustrative teaching samples, not externally assessed IELTS responses.

Illustrative · around Band 6.5

Social media companies should be responsible because their apps make people spend too much time online. Many people check their phones every few minutes, and this can affect their work and relationships. For example, students may look at notifications instead of studying. However, people should also control themselves because nobody forces them to use these applications. I think both sides have responsibility.

Illustrative · Band 8–9 qualities

Although users retain agency over their digital habits, platform operators should bear a substantial share of responsibility for design features that encourage prolonged engagement. Personalised notifications and continuously refreshed feeds can make disengagement difficult, particularly for younger users whose self-regulatory skills are still developing. A student who repeatedly interrupts study to respond to alerts illustrates how seemingly minor design choices can undermine sustained concentration. Nevertheless, regulation should target demonstrably harmful practices rather than remove individual choice altogether.

Compare the two versions

  • Precision: “design features that encourage prolonged engagement” identifies a mechanism more clearly than “apps make people spend too much time”.
  • Development: the stronger paragraph explains how notifications and feeds affect behaviour.
  • Qualification: “particularly for younger users” narrows the claim.
  • Example: the student example illustrates the mechanism rather than merely repeating the claim.
  • Critical thinking: the final sentence acknowledges the need to balance protection and autonomy.

Complexity alone does not create a high score. Relevance, development, precision and control matter.

07

Paragraph development workshop

Point → Explanation → Example → Implication

12 min
POINTMake one clear claim.
EXPLANATIONExplain why or how it happens.
EXAMPLEMake the mechanism concrete.
IMPLICATIONConnect it back to the question.

Worked example

Point: Digital platforms should take greater responsibility for protecting users from harmful design practices.

Explanation: Their interfaces are often optimised to sustain engagement, using notifications and personalised recommendations that can interrupt users' intended activities.

Example: A student who receives repeated alerts while preparing for an examination may find it difficult to maintain concentration, even when they initially intended to use the phone only for a practical purpose.

Implication: Since these design choices are controlled by platform operators, companies should be expected to reduce avoidable sources of distraction.

Exercise 1 · Diagnose the weak argument

“Social media is bad for students because they use it all the time. This is a big problem and schools should ban phones.”

  • Overgeneralisation: “students” and “all the time” are too broad.
  • Unsupported causal claim: no explanation of how use affects learning.
  • Unjustified solution: banning phones does not follow automatically from the claim.
  • Better approach: specify the context (e.g. lessons), mechanism (interruptions) and proportionate response (phone-free periods or classroom rules).

Exercise 2 · Improve a weak body paragraph

Rewrite this paragraph using at least three steps of the framework:

People should control their social media use. They waste time on it and do not do important things. For example, they use phones at work. This is bad for society.

Individuals also have a responsibility to regulate their use of social media, particularly when it interferes with professional or academic commitments. Because access to these platforms is voluntary in most circumstances, users can adopt practical strategies such as disabling non-essential notifications or setting limits on recreational browsing. For instance, an employee who checks social media during focused work periods may repeatedly interrupt tasks that require sustained attention. This suggests that, although platform design can encourage excessive use, personal self-regulation remains an important part of the solution.

Why it works: clear claim, explanation, concrete example and link to the debate.

Exercise 3 · Build your own paragraph

Basic idea: “Social media can help people feel less isolated.” Develop it using all four stages.

Social media can reduce feelings of isolation by helping individuals maintain relationships and find communities based on shared interests. This is particularly valuable for people who live far from family or have limited opportunities for face-to-face interaction. For example, international students may use online groups to exchange practical advice and establish friendships after moving abroad. Consequently, although excessive use can create problems, digital platforms should not be regarded as inherently harmful; their effects depend partly on how and why they are used.

09

Guided essay planning

Make decisions before writing. Do not copy a completed essay.

12 min

Return to the question

Some people believe that social media companies should be held responsible for the negative effects of their platforms on users' behaviour and mental well-being. Others argue that individuals should take responsibility for how they use these platforms. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Teacher conference

Before the student drafts, check: (1) both views are represented fairly; (2) the position is explicit; (3) the two body paragraphs have distinct controlling ideas; (4) each argument has a mechanism and support; (5) the counterargument is acknowledged without derailing the essay.

If the learner struggles, offer a choice of two positions: “shared responsibility, with stronger platform obligations” or “individual responsibility, with limited regulation of harmful design”. Ask them to defend one.
