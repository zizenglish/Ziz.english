Step 3 • Grammar & Collocations

Collocations with GO: go / go for / go on / go to / go to a / go to the

Study the 6 patterns with go, then match the meanings and fill in the gaps.

GO + -ing (go shopping, go sightseeing) GO FOR + a + noun (go for a walk, go for a coffee) GO ON + trip/tour (go on a cruise, go on holiday) GO TO + place/routine (go to school, go to work, go to Paris) GO TO A + event/place (go to a party, go to a café) GO TO THE + specific place (go to the hospital, go to the beach)

Unpopular opinion? Paris turned out to be the complete opposite of my expectations!

Before visiting Paris last month, my expectations were honestly pretty low. Half the internet told me the city was dirty, unfriendly, and stressful, so I wasn't sure what to expect. However, my 5-day trip turned out to be completely the opposite of what I had imagined!

First of all, the city was so much more friendly and clean than I thought it would be. Every café waiter we met was polite when we started with a simple "Bonjour!" Instead of spending all day inside museums, we went on an evening cruise along the River Seine and took the metro everywhere — super fast and easy. One morning, we even went cycling near the gardens!

Walking through neighbourhoods like Le Marais and Montmartre, I noticed how fashion-conscious and stylish local people are. Everything around looked so attractive — from little corner bakeries to historic bridges — and the whole vibe felt relaxed and welcoming. Honestly, I can't wait to go back!

Paris — The City of Light

Paris is the capital of France and one of the most famous cities in the world. It is located in the north-central part of the country along the river Seine, which divides the city into two main parts: the Left Bank and the Right Bank. Paris is divided into twenty administrative districts, or neighbourhoods, each with its own unique character and history.

Millions of tourists visit Paris every year to see its world-famous landmarks. The Eiffel Tower, built in 1889 as a temporary entrance arch for the World's Fair, has become the most iconic monument in France. Nearby stands the Louvre, the largest art museum on Earth and home to Leonardo da Vinci's masterpiece, the Mona Lisa. Another historic treasure is Notre-Dame Cathedral, which was badly damaged by a fire in April 2019 and carefully restored.

Getting around Paris is easy thanks to its fast underground railway system, the Métro, which opened in 1900. Just outside the city lies Disneyland Paris, which opened in 1992 — the park even has a secret network of underground tunnels so staff can move quickly between themed lands without spoiling the magic for guests!
