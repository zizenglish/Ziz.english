01 · EXAM STRATEGY

Know what the examiner is listening for

7 min

IELTS Speaking is a conversation, not a speech competition. Your job is to communicate ideas clearly and keep developing them.

Fluency & Coherence

Can you keep speaking at a natural pace, organise ideas and connect them logically?

Lexical Resource

Can you choose precise words, use collocations and paraphrase when a word is missing?

Grammatical Range & Accuracy

Can you use a mix of sentence structures and control them accurately?

Pronunciation

Can the listener understand you easily? Do stress, rhythm and intonation help your meaning?

High-value habit: Do not chase “difficult words”. A precise, natural phrase used accurately is better than an impressive word used incorrectly.

Three rules to remember

  1. Answer first. Make your position clear before adding explanation.
  2. Develop, don't decorate. Add a reason, example, contrast or consequence.
  3. Recover calmly. If you lose a word, paraphrase. Do not stop the whole answer.

02 · SPEAKING PART 1

Short questions. Strong answers.

11 min

Part 1 is a familiar-topic interview. Aim for 2–4 natural sentences. Avoid both one-word answers and long rehearsed speeches.

The 3-step answer: Direct answer → reason → small example/detail. Use only as much as the question needs.

Don't do this

“Yes. I like it.”

Do this

“Yes, definitely. It helps me switch off after a busy day, especially if I have been working at a screen for hours.”

Why it works

Clear position, reason, specific context. No unnecessary introduction.

Useful Part 1 language

To be honest,…What I enjoy most is…I tend to…It depends on…I'm quite keen on…I'm not particularly fond of…Compared with the past,…One thing I appreciate is…

Practice 1 · Listen, think, answer

The question is deliberately hidden. Listen carefully. You can replay it once. Answer aloud in 20–30 seconds.

Practice 2 · Repair the answer

Improve this answer by adding a reason and a concrete detail:

Exam hack: If you cannot think of an example, use a small personal detail. Specificity makes answers sound more convincing and helps you keep talking.

05 · SPEAKING PART 3

Think bigger. Explain better.

13 min

Part 3 moves from personal experience to general ideas. The examiner is interested in how you develop and explore a point, not whether your opinion is “correct”.

The IDEA ladder

  1. Idea: answer the question.
  2. Develop: explain why or how.
  3. Example: give a realistic illustration.
  4. Add nuance: compare, qualify or mention an exception.

Power phrases for deeper answers

One explanation might be…This tends to happen when…A case in point is…Compared with previous generations,…It would be an oversimplification to say…There are two sides to this.It largely depends on…In the long term,…Having said that,…One possible downside is…

Practice 1 · Listen and respond

The question appears only as audio. Aim for 40–50 seconds. Use the IDEA ladder.

Practice 2 · Add nuance

Make each statement more balanced. Write one improved sentence.

“Online learning is always better than classroom learning.”
“Young people don't read books anymore.”
Band-building habit: Avoid extreme claims such as “everyone”, “nobody” and “always” unless you can justify them. Qualifying language makes your reasoning more precise.
06 · PRONUNCIATION LAB

Make your meaning easy to follow

8 min

You do not need to remove your accent. Focus on intelligibility: clear sounds, word stress, sentence stress and thought groups.

Word stress

PHOtograph · phoTOgrapher · photoGRAPHic

Stress can change when a word changes form.

Sentence stress

“I REALLY enjoy travelling by TRAIN.”

Stress the words carrying the key information.

Chunking practice

Read aloud, pausing at the slashes. Then repeat with natural intonation.

Although I enjoy travelling alone / I sometimes prefer going with friends / because sharing an experience / makes it more memorable.

Shadowing routine

  1. Listen to the sentence using the button.
  2. Repeat immediately, copying rhythm rather than individual accent features.
  3. Record yourself and compare: are your pauses in meaningful places?

08 · REVIEW & HOMEWORK

Make progress visible

10 min

IELTS progress comes from noticing patterns and practising deliberately. Choose one priority for your next speaking session.

Self-assessment · Four criteria

Fluency & Coherence

Did I extend answers and organise ideas clearly?

Lexical Resource

Did I use precise vocabulary and paraphrase?

Grammar

Did I use varied structures accurately?

Pronunciation

Was I clear, easy to follow and appropriately stressed?

Homework · 35–45 minutes

Task 1 · Record a Part 2 answer

Describe a decision you made that had a positive effect on your life.

Say what the decision was, when you made it, what influenced you and why it was positive. Speak for 1–2 minutes.

Task 2 · Listen like a coach

  • Write down 3 strong expressions you used.
  • Identify 2 pauses, repetitions or unclear sentences.
  • Choose 1 pronunciation feature to practise.

Task 3 · Repeat

Record the same answer again after making improvements. Compare the two recordings. What changed?

Task 4 · Part 3 extension

Prepare spoken answers (40–60 seconds) to:

  1. Why do people sometimes regret important decisions?
  2. Should young people be encouraged to take risks?

Task 5 · Vocabulary notebook

Choose 10 chunks from this playbook. Add a definition or synonym and one original sentence for each.

Final life hack: Keep a weekly “repeat recording”. Answer a similar question every week. Improvement becomes much easier to hear when you compare recordings over time.
