Vocabulary Reference Table

Relationship Phrases: Meaning & Grammar Notes

Phrase Clear Explanation Grammar & Usage Note
get to know (someone) Spend time with someone so you gradually become friends. Past: got to know each other (process, not a single moment).
work closely together Collaborate a lot on the same tasks or projects at work. Adverb closely goes after the verb work.
have a baby Become parents / give birth to a child. Use have (not do/make): They recently had a baby.
get divorced Legally end a marriage. get divorced (from someone); state: be divorced.
get on (well) with Have a friendly, comfortable relationship with someone. Requires preposition with before a person: get on with my boss.
move in (next door / with) Start living in a new house, flat, or with a partner. Opposite: move out (leave a home).
share an apartment / flat Live in the same flat with flatmates and split the rent. share a flat with someone.
a big age gap / be close A large difference in years; close = having a strong emotional bond. There is a 10-year age gap between us.
be dating (for a while) Have a romantic relationship before marriage. Often used in Continuous: They've been dating for a year.

Stage 3A • Grammar Clarification

Word Order in Questions (QUASI / ASI Formula)

1. Main Verbs (Present / Past / Continuous / Perfect)

(Question word +) Auxiliary + Subject + Verb

• How do you travel to work?

• Do you know the person well?

2. Questions with "be" (No do/does/did!)

(Question word +) am / is / are / was / were + Subject

• What is your favourite drink?

• Are you a good friend to her?

Tense (Question word) Auxiliary Subject Verb
Present simple— / — / WhatDo / Do / doesyou / they / hework? / live near here? / do?
Past simple— / — / WhyDid / Did / didyou / they / heget home OK? / speak to you? / go there?
Present continuous— / — / WhereIs / Are / areshe / they / youvisiting? / driving here? / staying?
Present perfect— / — / How longHas / Have / haveshe / they / youdecided? / arrived? / lived here?
How QUASI Works (Step-by-Step Breakdown)
1. Question word Optional for Yes/No questions. Always first in Wh-questions (Where, Why, How long).
2. Auxiliary verb Helper verb showing tense: do/does (Present), did (Past), am/is/are (Continuous), have/has (Perfect), can/will/should (Modals).
3. Subject The person or thing doing the action (you, she, the train, your parents). Always goes after the auxiliary!
4. Infinitive / Main verb After do/does/did/can, always use the base form (V1) without -s or -ed!
❌ Common Student Mistake ✅ Correct Form & Rule
Where you live? Where do you live? (Missing auxiliary do)
What does she wants? What does she want? (No -s after does)
Did you saw him? Did you see him? (Use V1 see after did)
Do you are tired? Are you tired? (Never use do with be!)

Complete the Questions (1–10) & What vs. Which

WHAT (General / Unlimited)

What is the tallest building in the world? (There are many buildings.)

WHICH (Limited Choice: 2 or a few)

Which building is taller, Big Ben or the Eiffel Tower?

Question Word What It Asks About Example Question & Answer
What / What kind of Things, ideas, jobs / specific type or category What kind of music do you like? — Rock music.
Which Choice between a small, limited number of options Which tea do you prefer, green or black?
Who / Whose Person / Possession (owner — careful: Whose ≠ Who's!) Whose phone is this? — It's Magda's.
How long / How far Duration of time / Physical distance How long does it take? — 20 minutes. / How far is it? — 5 km.
How much / How many Uncountable nouns (time, money) / Plural countable nouns How much time do you spend online? / How many shoes do you own?
Prepositions at the end! In English, prepositions (to, with, for, about, from) stay at the end of the question. Who do you live with? / Where are you from? (Not: With who do you live?)

Stage 4 • Core Grammar Focus

Object Questions vs. Subject Questions

1. Object Questions (Use do / does / did)

Who did you have lunch with? → I had lunch with my boss.

The question word asks about the OBJECT (the receiver of the action). Normal question word order applies: Qw + do/does/did + Subject + V1.

✓ What did you see?  |  ✗ What saw you?

2. Subject Questions (NO do / does / did!)

Who called the bank? → Ben called the bank.

When Who / What / Which IS the subject (the person/thing doing the action), do NOT use do, does, or did. Keep positive statement word order!

✓ Who called the bank?  |  ✗ Who did call the bank?

Feature Object Question Subject Question
What is unknown? The object (we already know WHO did the action). The subject / doer (we don't know WHO/WHAT did it).
Formula Who / What + do / does / did + Subject + V1? Who / What + Verb (V1+s / V2 / has V3)?
Direct Contrast Pair 1 Who did Romeo love? (We know Romeo loved someone → Juliet) Who loved Juliet? (We want to know who did the loving → Romeo)
Direct Contrast Pair 2 What did you break? (You broke something → a glass) What broke the window? (Something broke it → the ball)
Quick Exam Trick Replace Who/What with "Someone/Something". If the sentence still makes sense in normal order ("Someone called the bank"), it is a Subject Question → NO do/does/did!

Are We Really So Different?

Do you ever look at people around you and say to yourself, 'Wow, we're all so different!'? Well, recently I had the opposite idea. I think that in many ways we're all the same. We like, feel and do many of the same things.

What kind of things do we all do at home? We all dance in the kitchen to our favourite music and we look in the fridge for no reason. We hide things. For example, we put a key behind a book on a shelf so that no one else can find it. We wake up early at weekends when we don't have college or work. We look at our phone in bed, just before we go to sleep.

What about outside the home? Nobody likes waiting, so it's not surprising that we all hate traffic and queues. In the cinema we all cry at the sad part of the film. We miss our friends when they go away. We join gyms and pay a lot of money to be a member, and then we never go.

Do you buy things and use them only once? Do you pack extra clothes in your suitcase when you go on holiday? Of course you do. We all do.
